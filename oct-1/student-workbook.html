Lead a discovery conversation

Andrew is developing a mentorship program connecting LMU students with peers who completed internships and received full-time return offers. We're exploring two needs: finding an internship and learning how to succeed during one. Interview someone about their actual experience: how they found help, what worked, and what remained difficult. What we learn will help shape the program.

For the internship capstone route, secure your internship by October 22.

Five moves for your seven-minute conversation

Move Language you can borrow
Open "I'd like to understand your experience with internships and getting guidance. We have seven minutes. I'll take notes and check my understanding at the end. Does that work?"
Understand Pick a starting question below that fits this person's experience. Ask one question at a time.
Explore "You mentioned ___. Could you walk me through that?" Follow their answer.
Confirm "Let me check my understanding. [Summarize.] What did I miss or get wrong?"
Close "What should we check next, with whom, and by when?" Agree on a real next step if useful; otherwise name an open question and thank them.

Choose a starting point

Their experience Start here
Seeking an internship "Tell me about your most recent attempt to find or apply for an internship." Then: "Where did you need help?"
Internship completed "Before your internship started, what did you wish you could ask someone who had already done it?" Then ask how they actually found help.
Return offer received "Walk me through your internship and how the return offer came about."
Has helped a peer "Tell me about the last time someone asked you for help with an internship or getting a return offer."
Search not started "What have you done so far to figure out where to begin?" It's okay if they haven't started.

When you need a follow-up or a reset

  • "What happened next?" / "How did you find that person?"
  • "Could you explain that part?" / "What advice did you use?"
  • "What feedback did you receive?" / "Before we move on, could we return to ___?"

Ask about existing LMU connections and where useful information was kept or shared. If someone suggests a feature, ask what experience prompted it. If a question doesn't fit, change it. You can pause and consult this guide.

Practice, feedback, retry

7 minutes interview · 2 feedback · 1 retry · 1 to record and rotate. Summarize when you hear the one-minute warning. The observer names one move that helped and one moment to try again, with examples. The participant corrects the account. The interviewer retries and records the feedback in their interview notes.

Use this as a guide. Follow the answer. You do not need to ask every question.
